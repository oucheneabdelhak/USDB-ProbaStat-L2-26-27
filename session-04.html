Chapter 2 · Session 4
Dispersion parameters

Range · mean absolute deviation · variance and standard deviation · coefficient of variation · boxplot and outliers

Today you will be able to:
  • quantify how far observations lie from the centre;
  • compute and interpret variance, standard deviation and coefficient of variation;
  • build and read a boxplot, and flag outliers with Tukey's rule.

Position alone describes a cloud by its centre. Dispersion tells us whether the cloud is dense or scattered.

1Same centre, different clouds

Two models, same mean accuracy
Model A: \(\bar X = 0.87\), \(\sigma = 0.002\) — stable.
Model B: \(\bar X = 0.87\), \(\sigma = 0.05\) — oscillates between \(0.81\) and \(0.93\).

Same mean, different engineering decision. Model B cannot be deployed without a variance-reduction effort.

Two services, same mean latency
Service A: every request served in \(55\) ms.
Service B: half served in \(10\) ms, half in \(100\) ms.

Both have a mean of \(55\) ms. They are not the same service.

What dispersion answers
A dispersion parameter answers one question: how far from the centre are the observations spread? Each parameter (range, MAD, variance, CV, IQR) uses a different notion of distance, and each has its own domain of validity.
The rule of the course
A single number is never sufficient. A report that gives only a mean, without a dispersion, describes the data through one aspect only — and usually the wrong one for the question asked.

2Range and mean absolute deviation

Range
\[ r \;=\; x_{\max} - x_{\min}. \]

Latency: \(80-30=50\) ms. Accuracy: \(0.874-0.865=0.009\).

Mean absolute deviation
\[ e_X \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i\,|x_i - \bar X|. \]

The average distance from the mean, in the unit of the character.

MAD on the ten accuracies (n=10, x̄=0.870)
Deviations in absolute value: \(0.005,0.002,0.001,0.001,0.001,0,0.001,0.002,0.003,0.004\). Their sum is \(0.020\), so \[ e_X \;=\; \frac{0.020}{10} \;=\; 0.002 . \] Interpretation: on average, a training run is off by two thousandths of accuracy from the mean.
Strength of MAD
  • Same unit as the character → directly interpretable.
  • Robust: one outlier does not dominate.
Weakness of MAD
  • The absolute value is not differentiable → no decomposition.
  • Does not combine linearly when samples are merged.
  • Cannot be extended to a covariance / correlation.

This is why the next parameter replaces \(|x_i - \bar X|\) by \((x_i-\bar X)^2\).

Weakness of the range
The range depends on only two observations — the most extreme ones. A single outlier destroys it. It is useful as a measure of the total extent of the data (for choosing histogram bounds), not as a measure of dispersion.

3Variance and standard deviation

Variance
\[ \operatorname{Var}(X) = \sigma_X^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i\,(x_i - \bar X)^2 . \]
Standard deviation
\[ \sigma_X \;=\; \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)} . \]

In the same unit as the character — this is why it is preferred in reports.

König's formula — one-pass computation
\[ \operatorname{Var}(X) \;=\; \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{k} n_i x_i^2 \;-\; \bar X^{\,2} \;=\; \overline{x^2} - \bar x^2 . \]

The variance is the mean of the squares minus the square of the mean. No need to know \(\bar X\) first — the formula is valid in a single pass and extends to grouped data by replacing \(x_i\) with the class centre.

4Properties of the variance

Four properties used constantly
  1. \(\operatorname{Var}(X) \ge 0\), with equality iff all observations are equal.
  2. Invariance by translation: \(\operatorname{Var}(X+b) = \operatorname{Var}(X)\).
  3. Homogeneity of degree two: \(\operatorname{Var}(aX) = a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)\), hence \(\sigma_{aX+b} = |a|\,\sigma_X\).
  4. Combination of disjoint samples: \[ \operatorname{Var}(X) \;=\; \frac{n_1\operatorname{Var}(X_1)+n_2\operatorname{Var}(X_2)}{n_1+n_2} \;+\; \frac{n_1 n_2}{(n_1+n_2)^2}(\bar X_1 - \bar X_2)^2 . \]
Property (iii) in action
A latency \(X\) has \(\sigma_X = 10.34\) ms. Convert to seconds: \(Y = X/1000\), so \(\sigma_Y = 10.34/1000 \approx 0.0103\) s.

The standard deviation transforms like a length, not like a squared length — which is why σ, not σ², is reported in engineering.

Property (iv) in action
Two regions serve a service: region 1 (\(n_1=60\), \(\bar X_1=40\), \(\sigma_1=5\)) and region 2 (\(n_2=40\), \(\bar X_2=70\), \(\sigma_2=8\)).

Total variance = weighted within-variance + between-group variance: \[ \frac{60\cdot 25 + 40\cdot 64}{100} + \frac{60\cdot 40}{100^2}(40-70)^2 = 40.6 + 216 \approx 257 . \] The between term dominates: the two regions are far apart, so most of the total variance is the difference of the means, not the dispersion inside each region.

Why property (iv) matters
It is the seed of the analysis of variance and of the decomposition of the variance of a bivariate series into "within-group" and "between-group" parts. Every chapter of inferential statistics uses it.

5Coefficient of variation

Definition
For a character with \(\bar X > 0\) and standard deviation \(\sigma_X\): \[ CV_X \;=\; \frac{\sigma_X}{\bar X}. \] Often expressed as a percentage: \(CV_X = 100\,\sigma_X/\bar X\). It is dimensionless.
Three characters, three units — σ alone cannot compare them

Conventions of interpretation
\(CV \le 0.25\): homogeneous  ·  \(0.25 < CV \le 0.80\): moderately scattered  ·  \(CV > 0.80\): very heterogeneous. These are conventions, not theorems; they give a common vocabulary to reports.
When CV is meaningless
It requires \(\bar X > 0\), which excludes characters centred on zero or taking negative values (returns, errors). It is also meaningless if the zero of the scale is arbitrary (temperature in °C, relative score differences).

7Outliers and Tukey's 1.5·IQR rule

Definition — the fences
\[ L = Q_1 - k \cdot IQR, \qquad U = Q_3 + k \cdot IQR . \] Standard convention: \(k = 1.5\). The whiskers extend from \(Q_1\) to the smallest observation \(\ge L\), and from \(Q_3\) to the largest observation \(\le U\). Any observation strictly outside \([L, U]\) is drawn as an outlier.
Try it. Move the multiplier \(k\) and watch the fences on three endpoints.
1.5

At \(k = 1.5\) (Tukey's convention), the health-check endpoint has no outlier, the search endpoint has 1, and the recommendation endpoint flags its heavy right tail.

An outlier is not an error
The rule flags atypical observations for inspection; it does not say they are wrong. Deleting an outlier without examination is a form of data manipulation. Report it, inspect it, document what you did with it — do not silently drop it.

Five practical rules
  1. Symmetric data, no outliers → mean and standard deviation.
  2. Skewed data, or heavy tails → median and IQR; report the mean alongside, not instead.
  3. Nominal character → mode only (no mean, no median).
  4. Ratios or rates → geometric or harmonic mean; the arithmetic mean is the wrong average.
  5. Comparison across units → coefficient of variation.

✓Summary of Session 4

Dispersion parameters at a glance
  • Range \(r = x_{\max}-x_{\min}\) — total extent, sensitive to one value.
  • MAD \(e_X = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i n_i|x_i-\bar X|\) — typical distance, robust, same unit.
  • Variance \(\sigma_X^2 = \tfrac{1}{n}\sum_i n_i(x_i-\bar X)^2 = \overline{x^2}-\bar x^2\) (König) — the workhorse of inference.
  • Standard deviation \(\sigma_X = \sqrt{\sigma_X^2}\) — in the unit of the character, transforms as \(\sigma_{aX+b}=|a|\sigma_X\).
  • Coefficient of variation \(CV_X = \sigma_X/\bar X\) — dimensionless; used to compare dispersions across units.
  • IQR \(= Q_3 - Q_1\) — robust spread; the box in the boxplot.
  • Boxplot — superimposes \(x_{\min}, Q_1, Me, Q_3, x_{\max}\) and flags outliers beyond \(Q_{1/3} \mp 1.5\,IQR\).
Common mistakes
  • Confusing mean and median on a skewed distribution — the median is the honest summary; the mean must accompany it, not replace it.
  • Comparing standard deviations across units — \(10\) ms is small for a latency, large for an accuracy. Use CV.
  • Treating outliers as errors — the Tukey rule flags for inspection, not for deletion.
  • Reading a boxplot as a histogram — the box contains \(50\%\) of the observations, but its area does not represent that fraction.
  • Computing a variance from a single observation — variance is a property of a spread, not of a point.
  • Forgetting the between-group term when merging samples — property (iv) of the variance is not optional.
